# REP LOG

One line per rep. Append to the bottom. Never backfill, never catch up.

**Why there are no pre-filled dates:** overtime destroys schedules, and empty rows waiting to
be filled would imply you owe them. You don't. The count of reps is the pace — not how many
days went by.

**Day type** is what the day turned out to be, written after the fact: `floor` · `shift` ·
`off` (rotation off day) · `deep` (one of the 6).

## How to read this at the end of a set

Two questions, and this table answers both:

- **Am I on pace?** Count the reps. A set is ~16 days. Anything above about ten reps is a set
  that worked, regardless of how big any single one was.
- **What did I skip?** Look at which worksheet pages never appear in the Page column. A page
  that never got touched is the thing to open first on your next deep day.

**A floor rep counts the same as a deep one in the count.** That's deliberate. The streak
measures whether you showed up, not how much you produced — showing up on a 72 is worth more
than a big day when you were fresh.
