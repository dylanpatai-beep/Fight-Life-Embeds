## 1. THE THROUGH-LINE

> learn it → apply it → teach it in the gym → publish it → the audience it earns funds the exit

Three surfaces, one loop:

- **LEARN** — 8 subjects, each a gated sequence of modules.
- **TRACK** — one log: training, camp, module completion, the daily action.
- **CREATE** — every module ships something public. The curriculum *is* the content calendar.

**The load-bearing claim:** you are not learning these subjects. You already do most of
them. What you cannot yet do at will is *decompose them, teach them, and publish them.*
That gap is the entire business. A fighter who can only fight has one income. A fighter who
can break down what he does, teach it, and put it on the internet has four.

This reframe matters because it fixes an otherwise fatal problem with the curriculum: you
are an experienced fighter and a working paramedic. A module that says "learn the switch
kick" is insulting and useless. A module that says "be able to teach the switch kick to a
stranger in twenty minutes, and prove it" is hard, useful, and directly monetizable.

**Every module in this spec is a teaching-and-shipping module, not a beginner module.**

## 3. UNIT ANATOMY — WHAT A MODULE IS

**A module is one teachable idea.** Not a topic. Not a book. Something you could stand up
and teach in twenty minutes.

If it takes longer than twenty minutes to teach, it's two modules.

### Contents, in fixed order

| # | Part | What it is | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Header** | Module ID, title, one-line scope, **apply period (named explicitly)**, output channel + format | Worksheet p.1 |
| 2 | **Sources** | 2–4 named inputs. Book chapter, video, coach conversation, a session in the room, a paper. Hard cap at 4. | Worksheet p.2 |
| 3 | **Study notes** | What you actually took from each source | Worksheet p.2 |
| 4 | **Distillation** | "If I had five minutes to teach this" — the compression step. This is where learning actually happens. | Worksheet p.3 |
| 5 | **Teach-back** | Taught to a real human. Logged: who, when, what didn't land. | Worksheet p.4 |
| 6 | **Application** | The module's own named period. Start date, end date, what changed. | Worksheet p.5 + tracker |
| 7 | **Output** | The public artifact. Hook, structure, URL once shipped. | Worksheet p.6 |

### The gate — **[LOCKED]**, your three conditions

A module is **DONE** when all three are true:

- **G1** — worksheet + notes complete
- **G2** — taught to someone (name + date logged)
- **G3** — applied for the module's defined period (start + end dates both recorded)

### Shipping closes it — **[LOCKED]** 2026-08-09

**The three conditions make a module DONE. Shipping the output CLOSES it and opens the
next one.** A done-but-unshipped module blocks the sequence.

That's deliberate: the thing that stops your progress is an unpublished draft — exactly the
failure mode a solo creator needs pressure against.

#### The private-subject variant — **[LOCKED]** 2026-08-09

Subjects 5–8 are `private (post-FD)`. They have nothing to publish, so a naive reading of
the rule would deadlock them forever.

**For private subjects, the output is MADE but not PUBLISHED.** Written, drafted, filmed —
finished to the same standard, committed to the repo, and held. The module closes on the
artifact existing, not on it going out.

> "Nothing gets learned that doesn't get **shipped**" becomes
> "nothing gets learned that doesn't get **made**."

The intent survives — you still have to compress it into something a stranger could use,
which is where the learning actually happens. Only distribution waits. When you leave the
department, there's a back catalogue sitting there ready, not a blank page.

### Gating rules

- Modules within a subject are **strictly sequential.** No skipping ahead.
- **Teach-back and output can be the same event.** Teach it at the gym, film it, that's your
  short. Use this constantly — it's the highest-leverage shortcut in the system.
- **Apply periods run in parallel with the next module's study.** Otherwise the curriculum
  stalls for weeks doing nothing. Module N's application overlaps Module N+1's sources and
  notes; only the *gate check* is sequential.
- **Per-module apply periods** — your call, and it's the right one, but it means the period
  is easy to forget to set. Mitigation: it's in the header block, page one, above the fold.
  A worksheet with a blank apply period is an invalid worksheet.

### The worksheet — fixed structure, every module, forever

Six pages. Same every time. You print it, fill it by hand in GoodNotes, upload the pages
back.

The structure is fixed so that **you never have to learn a new worksheet.** After a 72 you
open page 3 and you know what page 3 is.

**Every page ends with one line:**

> **STOPPED AT: ________________**

That line is the entire cold-resume mechanism. It is not optional. It is the single most
important design element in this spec, and it costs five seconds.

## 5. PACING — BUILT ON THE SET, NEVER THE WEEK

### The unit of time

**One set ≈ 16 days:** 10-day rotation (five 24-hour shifts, alternating) + 6 consecutive
days off. There is no Monday in this system. Nothing in this spec references a week.

### The daily action — **the same every day, forever**

> **One rep on the current module.**
> Open the worksheet. Do the next unchecked line. Write STOPPED AT. Close it.

That is the whole thing. On shift, off shift, after a 72, during camp — **the action never
changes and you never select a mode.**

You said: *if the system requires me to remember which mode I'm in, it has already failed.*
So the four modes below are **not something you choose.** They are what a rep tends to look
like on that kind of day. You discover the mode by doing the rep, not by deciding in
advance. The system never asks you what day it is.

### What a rep tends to look like

| Day type | Duration | What one rep gets you | Design constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Floor** (post-48/72, or wrecked) | ≤5 min | One line. One source named. One field filled. | **Still counts. Streak intact.** This is the whole point of floor mode. |
| **Shift day** (on duty, 24h) | 15–30 min | One worksheet field, one source pass, or a set of script bullets | Abandonable mid-sentence. No setup, no build env, no focus block. Paper and a pen, or GoodNotes on the iPad. |
| **Rotation off day** (the 24 between shifts) | ~45 min | One worksheet section complete, or one teach-back at the gym | Recovery day. Half capacity assumed, not full. |
| **Deep day** (one of the 6 off) | 2–4 hrs | Module advancement: filming, editing, close-out, opening the next module | The only reliable deep-work window. Heavy building lives here and nowhere else. |

### What actually moves on which day

- **Shift days** carry the reading and the worksheet fields. Fragmented, interruptible,
  resumable cold. Nothing on a shift day requires a computer.
- **The 6 off** carry everything with setup cost: filming, editing, the teach-back if it
  needs to be filmed, module close-out, opening the next module.
- **Nothing is scheduled on a specific date.** Overtime destroys dates. The system only ever
  asks for the next rep.

### Overtime, camp, and the things that break schedules

- **A 48 or 72** → floor mode, automatically, by doing a smaller rep. No mode switch, no
  catch-up debt. **There is no catch-up in this system.** Catch-up is what kills streaks.
- **Fight camp** → **none scheduled as of 2026-08-09 [LOCKED].** Full cadence runs until one
  appears. When it does: the whole curriculum drops to floor mode for the duration. Camp is
  the application, not a competitor to it. Camp itself is tracked in tfl-app, which already
  handles it.
- **A missed day** → the streak counts *reps*, not days. You do not owe yesterday anything.

### The arithmetic, honestly

One module per set = **~23 modules per year** (365 ÷ 16).

Martial arts, as sequenced above, is 8 modules ≈ **11 sets ≈ 6 months** (several modules
carry 2-set apply periods).

Eight subjects × 8 modules = 64 modules ≈ **2.8 years at one module per set.**

Started now, that finishes in **mid-2029** — *after* your 2028 vesting date.

**This is the most important number in the document.** Two readings, and you have to pick:

1. **Run subjects sequentially** (one active at a time, in your numbered order). Martial
   arts and Fitness are done well before 2028. Subjects 5–8 arrive after the exit — which
   may be completely fine, since the exit doesn't end the learning.
2. **Cut the list.** Eight gated subjects with three-part gates may be more curriculum than
   one person with five 24-hour shifts per set can carry. See §7.

**My read:** option 1, and stop worrying about subjects 5–8. The exit is funded by depth in
