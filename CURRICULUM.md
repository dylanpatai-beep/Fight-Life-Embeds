**Version:** v0.1
**Rule:** Learning that produces no receipt did not happen.

---

## 0. The premise

A list of ten topics is a **menu**, not a curriculum. A curriculum needs four things a
menu doesn't have:

1. **Sequence** — what comes first, and what it unlocks
2. **Depth target** — how far into this topic you actually intend to go
3. **Evidence** — what proves you learned it
4. **A forcing function** — something external that fails if you don't do the work

You already have #4 and most people never do. The Fight Life is the forcing function.
A published piece is a deadline with an audience attached. This entire system is built
on that: **you learn by owing the internet an explanation.**

## 2. Depth ladder

You are not going to master ten things. Decide the ceiling per topic up front — this
is the single highest-leverage decision in the whole system.

| Level | Definition | Rough cost |
|---|---|---|
| **L1 · LITERATE** | Know the map and the vocabulary. Can hold a real conversation. | ~10 hrs |
| **L2 · APPLIED** | Can do the thing unsupervised in normal conditions. | ~50–100 hrs |
| **L3 · PRACTITIONER** | Can coach someone else through it. Handles edge cases. | ~300+ hrs |
| **L4 · AUTHORITY** | Produces original work. Other people cite you. | Years |

### Target ceilings

| Topic | Now → Target |
|---|---|
| Martial Arts | → **L4** (this is the life) |
| Paramedicine | → **L3** (the job funds the life) |
| Fitness / S&C | → **L3** |
| Content + AI | → **L3** (the engine must be strong) |
| Nutrition | → **L2** |
| Psychology | → **L2** |
| Business | → **L2** |
| Music / Art / Dance | → **L1–L2** |
| Travel / Language | → **L2** (one language, not five) |
| Culture | → **L1**, ongoing |

Aiming L1 at something is not failure. It's the decision that makes L4 possible
somewhere else.

## 3. The three lanes

The reason ten-topic curricula die: all ten get opened at once, none get closed.
At any moment topics live in exactly one of three lanes.

### LANE A — DEEP · one topic at a time
A 12-week cycle. ~5 hrs/week of deliberate study. Produces a **flagship artifact**.
Rotates every quarter. Only one topic is ever in Lane A.

### LANE B — WORKING · two topics
Things already inside your daily life — training, the job, food. No additional study
time is allocated. The work is **documentation**, not study: capture what happened,
compress it, ship it. Cheapest content you will ever make.

### LANE C — AMBIENT · everything else
Podcasts on the drive, curiosity rabbit holes, books before bed. **Capture only, zero
obligation.** Nothing in Lane C is allowed to generate guilt. Its only job is to feed
the inbox so that when a topic rotates into Lane A you're not starting cold.

Four quarters a year × 3 years = 12 deep cycles. Every topic gets one, the important
ones get two. That is a real curriculum with a real end date.

### Suggested rotation

| Quarter | Lane A (Deep) | Lane B (Working) |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Content Creation w/ AI | Martial Arts · Paramedicine |
| Q2 | Fitness / S&C · body mechanics | Martial Arts · Nutrition |
| Q3 | Paramedicine (patho + pharm) | Martial Arts · Fitness |
| Q4 | Psychology | Martial Arts · Nutrition |

**Start with Content Creation w/ AI.** Not because it's the most interesting — because
it's the multiplier. Every cycle after it ships faster, and you'd be studying it *by*
documenting martial arts and the job, so nothing gets paused.

## 4. The weekly loop

Five moves. This is the engine. Everything else is scaffolding.

```
CAPTURE  →  INTERROGATE  →  COMPRESS  →  SHIP  →  REVIEW
 daily        2×/week        1×/week    1×/week   weekly
  5 min        45 min         30 min      —        20 min
```

### CAPTURE · daily, 5 min
Everything lands in **one inbox**. Voice note in the car after training. Photo of a
notebook page. Link. Screenshot. No sorting, no tagging, no deciding — the entire
skill here is *not thinking*. Sorting at capture time is why capture systems die.

### INTERROGATE · 2×/week, 45 min → **NotebookLM**
Load your sources into the topic's notebook and attack them with questions. NotebookLM
is right for this specific job because it's grounded in *your* sources and cites them —
you can check every claim against the page it came from. Generate the Audio Overview
and put it on in the car; that's a second pass for free.

Questions that work better than "summarize this":
- *What does this source assume that it never states?*
- *Where do these two sources actually disagree?*
- *What would a skeptic in this field say about chapter 4?*
- *Give me the 5 questions I should be able to answer to prove I understand this.*
- *What's the practitioner's version of this vs. the textbook's version?*

### COMPRESS · 1×/week, 30 min → **you, then Claude**
Write **one page, in your own words, from memory, without the sources open.** This is
where learning actually happens. Everything before this is input; this is the first
moment your brain does the work.

Then hand it to Claude in the pillar Project: *"Here's my one-pager on X. Don't rewrite
it. Find what I got wrong, what I hand-waved, and what I left out that a practitioner
would consider essential."* Claude grades it. You fix it. **Claude does not write it** —
the second you let it write the compression you've automated away the only step that
was making you smarter.

### SHIP · 1×/week → **one public receipt**
Reel, carousel, thread, short, whatever. Teaching in public is the final exam, and it
is the only step that compounds outside your own head. A compression that never ships
is a diary entry.

### REVIEW · weekly 20 min, quarterly 90 min
Reread **your own compressions**, not other people's notes. Weekly: last 4. Quarterly:
the whole cycle, then pick the next Lane A topic and write its syllabus.

## 5. Module spec — the syllabus template

Fill this in **once per Lane A cycle**, in week 1. Not for all ten topics up front —
that's the trap. Write the syllabus for the topic you're actually about to do.

```markdown
# MODULE: [topic]
CYCLE:        Q_ 20__          PILLAR: [ ]
CURRENT:      L_               TARGET: L_
WHY NOW:      [one sentence — what in the life this unlocks]

## SOURCE STACK (hard cap: 5)
1. TEXTBOOK      — the canonical/systematic one
2. PRACTITIONER  — someone who actually does it, not just teaches it
3. COURSE/VIDEO  — for the motor/visual parts
4. PRIMARY       — a paper, a manual, a protocol, an original text
5. COMMUNITY     — a forum, a coach, a person you can ask

## UNITS (4 × 2 weeks + synthesis)
U1 — Question it answers: ____   Artifact: ____   Drill: ____
U2 — ...
U3 — ...
U4 — ...

## EVIDENCE OF COMPLETION
[the flagship artifact — the thing that proves L_ was reached]

## CONTENT ANGLES (3 per unit = 12–18 pieces)
U1: hook / myth to break / the thing nobody tells you
U2: ...
```

**The source cap is the point.** Five sources you actually finish beats forty you
collect. Collecting sources feels exactly like learning and is not.

### 12-week cycle shape

| Week | Work |
|---|---|
| **1** | Survey. Build the source stack. Write the syllabus. Ship: "I'm learning X, here's the map." |
| **2–9** | Four units, two weeks each. Weekly loop runs the whole time. |
| **10** | Synthesis. Build the flagship artifact. |
| **11** | Teach it. Long-form: video, essay, or a full carousel series. |
| **12** | Review. Score yourself against the level definition. Pick the next topic. |

One cycle should produce **~18–24 content pieces**. That's the whole thesis: learning
and content are not two activities competing for your time. Study *is* production.

## 6. Tool routing

The mistake is asking "which AI tool should I use." The right question is **which room
am I in.** Each tool is a room with one job.

| Tool | Room | Job | Don't use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NotebookLM** | The classroom | Interrogating sources you loaded. Cited answers. Audio overviews for the car. | Drafting content — it's grounded in sources, not in your voice |
| **Claude Projects** | The studio + the coach | Drafting in your voice, Socratic drilling, grading compressions, syllabus design | Fact-heavy source Q&A where citation matters |
| **Notion** | The transcript | Curriculum DB, lane status, receipts log. Already your Command Center. | Thinking. It's a record, not a workspace |
| **Claude Code + this repo** | The shipping surface | Public embeds, trackers, pages | Study |
| **Voice memo → transcript** | The field | Post-training and post-shift downloads while it's hot | — |

**NotebookLM setup:** one notebook per Lane A/B topic, max ~5 live at a time.
Retire a notebook when the cycle closes — don't accumulate.

**Claude Project setup:** one per pillar, matching your existing L2 map. Each carries
PATAI OS + your voice guide + the current module spec in project knowledge. Then the
Project already knows the context every time, and you stop re-explaining yourself.

**Notion curriculum DB fields:**
`Topic · Pillar · Lane (A/B/C) · Current Level · Target Level · Cycle · Source Stack ·
Compressions (count) · Receipts Shipped · Flagship Artifact · Next Action`

## 7. On building the app

Don't. Not yet.

Building a learning app is the most satisfying way to avoid learning. It *feels* like
the highest-leverage move and it is reliably the lowest — you'd spend the first cycle
building a container instead of putting anything in one. And you would build the wrong
thing, because you don't yet know what the system needs; you'd be designing from
imagination instead of from friction.

**The rule:** the app gets built after **two completed 12-week cycles** — and only for
the specific friction those cycles actually surfaced. By then you'll know exactly what
it does, because you'll have felt what's missing instead of guessing.

Meanwhile the "app" already exists and takes a weekend to assemble:

- **Notion DB** — the curriculum registry
- **NotebookLM** — one notebook per active topic
- **Claude Projects** — one per pillar
- **This repo** — the public-facing pages
- **Voice memos** — capture

And here's the part worth sitting with: **the app is a content topic, not a
prerequisite.** Building it in public is a Lane A cycle under #9 and #10 — every
session is a receipt. You get the app *and* the content, but only if you sequence it
that way instead of doing it first and silently.

Three. Nothing about hours consumed, videos watched, or books started.

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| **Compressions written** | 1 / week |
| **Receipts shipped** | 1 / week |

Hours consumed is a vanity metric. It measures how long you sat near information.

## 9. Failure modes

| Trap | What it looks like | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Collector's fallacy** | 40 saved sources, 0 finished | Hard cap of 5. Finish before adding. |
| **Consumption without receipt** | Great week, nothing shipped | Ship rule is non-negotiable. Ugly and shipped beats polished and queued. |
| **All lanes hot** | All 10 topics "in progress" | One Lane A. Two Lane B. That's it. |
| **System restarts** | Rebuilding the system instead of running it | The system is done. It's this file. Run it. |
| **Tool-building** | Learning is paused while infra gets built | See §7. |
| **AI does the compressing** | Claude writes the one-pager, you feel productive | You write it. Claude grades it. Non-negotiable. |
